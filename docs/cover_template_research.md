# Cover Template Research: 2025–2026 Genre Conventions

Research brief for building 12–15 parametric book-cover layout templates for Atmosphere Press's automated cover designer. Compiled 2026-08-28 from current bestseller-list coverage, cover-design trade press, designer blogs, and BookTok trend coverage. This is a **text-only research pass** (no image access) — every claim below is drawn from written descriptions in the sources listed at the end, not from viewing the covers directly. Where a description is thin, confidence is marked down accordingly rather than inferred from memory of what these books "probably" look like.

## How to read this document

18 conventions are documented below (aim was 14–18; author should pick the strongest 12–15). Each includes a `key`, the subject-key genre(s) it serves, named evidence, and enough layout/type/art/palette/legibility detail to brief an image-generation prompt and a text-layout template without seeing a single reference image. `mystery_thriller` covers two genuinely distinct sub-conventions (thriller vs. cozy) even though it's one subject key in the press's taxonomy — both are included and labeled.

## 1. `romantasy_emblem`

**genres:** fantasy, romantasy

**evidence:** Rebecca Yarros's *Fourth Wing* / *Onyx Storm* (Empyrean series, design by Bree Archer & Elizabeth Turner Stokes) — a circular sunburst frame with nested rune-like border rings and a central dragon emblem in black and gold; Reedsy's dedicated "Romantasy Book Covers" resource describes the category pattern as a "tarot-card composition" — a central figure or motif inside an arched ornamental frame with hands, moons, stars, or runes arranged around it; multiple 2025–2026 trend pieces (SPINE, MIBLART) note gold foil and ornate borders are "practically a category convention now."

**layout:** Title dead-center or upper-third, huge (35–50% of cover height), stacked 1–3 lines, caps or small caps. Author name small, centered directly below the title, tight tracking. Composition is symmetrical, organized in concentric rings around one central emblem in the middle third — never off-axis.

**type:** Cormorant Garamond (best fit — delicate, high-contrast, reads as "engraved" at display size) primary; Playfair Display second choice for a harder-edged series look. Title in caps/small-caps with loose tracking (engraved-metal feel); author name tighter and smaller in the same family.

**art:** One central emblem — mythical creature, weapon crossed with flora, crown, moon-and-hands motif — as flat/vector or fine linework illustration (not painterly-photoreal), enclosed in a circular or pointed-arch frame with nested decorative bands (rope-twist, rune, floral-vine). Leave a clean ring of flat background between the emblem and outer border, plus a clear horizontal band above and below the emblem for title/author — the emblem must not touch the top or bottom edge.

**palette:** Dark jewel-tone ground (midnight blue, deep emerald, oxblood, black) with one metallic accent (gold or silver) tracing the linework and title.

**scrim/legibility habit:** Title sits in a flat color band or directly on the dark ground, not over textured art — legibility comes from metallic-on-dark contrast, and the emblem stays inside its own ring so it never runs behind the type.

**confidence:** High — corroborated by the market's single biggest romantasy franchise plus multiple independent trend write-ups describing the identical "tarot-card emblem in ornate frame" pattern.

## 2. `fantasy_painterly_worldscape`

**genres:** fantasy

**evidence:** "The Honorable Con" (Reedsy, *20 Best Book Covers of 2025*) — an ornate ruby-red train against a luminous green background, conveying momentum through an otherworldly landscape; The Rainbow Stories, "Top Fantasy Book Cover Trends in 2025" — symbolic imagery (e.g., a crown, "cracked and melting") and dense, intentional illustration with borders, patterns, and hidden motifs that reward closer inspection.

**layout:** Title in the lower third or across the horizon line, moderate-to-huge, centered, usually caps, with full-bleed artwork behind/around it — no containing border (this is a full "world" scene, not a contained icon, which is what separates it from the emblem template). Author name smallest, bottom edge.

**type:** Playfair Display (best fit — bold, dramatic, reads "epic" at scale) primary; Cormorant Garamond second choice for a more delicate, literary-leaning epic fantasy. Short all-caps title, loose tracking; no script.

**art:** One evocative landscape or object-scene (a floating structure, a cracked crown, a distant tower, a creature mid-flight), painted or illustrated with visible brushwork or vector linework — dense with small details for close inspection but bold-shaped enough to read at thumbnail size. Reserve a clear sky/horizon band (upper or lower third) sized to hold the title without competing linework behind it.

**palette:** Saturated but moody — one dominant hue (forest green, storm blue, rust) against a luminous complementary glow (amber, coral) at the horizon or light source.

**scrim/legibility habit:** A soft atmospheric gradient (fog, glow, light bloom) built into the illustration itself behind the text zone — diegetic, not a pasted-on rectangle.

**confidence:** Medium-high — well-supported by a named 2025 "best cover" example and multiple trend pieces, but epic fantasy currently splits its allegiance between this and the emblem-frame look, so it's not singularly dominant the way the emblem is.

## 3. `romantasy_character_portrait`

**genres:** romantasy

**evidence:** AIBookArt, "Book Cover Design Trends in 2026" — notes "character portraits in detailed settings" and "detailed character illustrations" replacing generic photomanipulation across fantasy romance; general pattern in 2026 trend coverage of illustrated figures becoming more rendered (soft shading, painterly texture) rather than flat.

**layout:** A bust/half-figure illustrated portrait of the protagonist(s), centered or left-third if depicting a couple (leaving the right third for type); title stacked in the remaining third, moderate-to-huge scale.

**type:** Cormorant Garamond primary, Playfair Display second choice — same family logic as `romantasy_emblem` so a press can run the two as a matched sub-series.

**art:** An illustrated (painterly or detailed-vector, never photoreal) close/medium portrait in costume, with a small symbolic motif (weapon, familiar creature, piece of jewelry) worked into the background. Hold negative space in a vertical third beside or below the figure; the face/eyes must never be crossed by title type.

**palette:** Same jewel-tone-plus-metallic family as the emblem template, warmed slightly by skin-tone-adjacent highlights since a figure is present.

**scrim/legibility habit:** A soft vignette or cloak/fabric shape behind the shoulder darkens toward the text zone — contrast without a visible hard-edged box.

**confidence:** Medium — a real and growing pattern, but the sourcing is thinner and more generic than the emblem convention (no specific named 2025 bestseller confirms this exact portrait layout in text), so treat as the secondary romantasy option, not the primary bet.

## 4. `scifi_cosmic_vista`

**genres:** science_fiction

**evidence:** General but consistent space-opera description across sources ("ships against nebulae, planetary arcs, a figure small against the vastness"); *Nebula Awards Showcase 58* (2025) as a genre-representative current release; continued cultural visibility of the astronaut-against-space convention via the *Project Hail Mary* film/book tie-in wave (cover artist Will Staehle).

**layout:** Title centered in the lower third or across the bottom edge, huge, caps, sitting at/below a planet or nebula's horizon; author name smaller, bottom-most. A small silhouetted figure or ship sits off-center (rule-of-thirds) for scale contrast — never centered behind the title.

**type:** Orbitron (best fit — geometric, squared-off, unmistakably technological at display size) primary; Spectral second choice for softer, more literary/character-driven science fiction that wants less hard-SF signaling.

**art:** A vast cosmic scene — nebula, planetary curve, star field — with one small silhouetted figure or vessel for scale, as flat/gradient illustration (glowing color-blend nebulae, not photoreal NASA-style imagery). Negative space is the "empty space" itself in the upper two-thirds, naturally open enough to hold a large title.

**palette:** Electric blue/purple/magenta gradient space tones, occasionally offset with one warm accent (orange planet-glow, a single warning-yellow light).

**scrim/legibility habit:** Title sits in the darkest, least-detailed part of the gradient (the "empty" region), not over a bright nebula core — legibility by placement, not an added scrim shape.

**confidence:** Medium — a durable, evergreen sci-fi baseline rather than a sharply new 2025–2026 shift; sources describe it as continuing rather than freshly dominant, making it the weaker of the two science-fiction picks here.

## 5. `scifi_geometric_object_minimal`

**genres:** science_fiction

**evidence:** Creative Paramita / DAMONZA on "thumbnail simplicity with hidden detail" and bold-shape, high-contrast approaches specifically called out for sci-fi covers; AIBookArt 2026 trends note a retro 70s/80s sci-fi aesthetic resurgence; general minimalist-icon pattern associated with upmarket/literary science fiction.

**layout:** Title huge, dead-center or upper-third, one or two words, tight-tracked caps; one small iconic object (a satellite, a single porthole, an isolated structure) centered below or behind the type, everything else flat empty color.

**type:** Orbitron primary (its geometric letterforms double as "the icon" even as plain text); EB Garamond or Spectral second choice when the book leans literary/upmarket rather than pulp-technical.

**art:** One bold, simplified graphic object (spacecraft silhouette, planet disc, a warped grid line suggesting a portal) as a flat vector shape with at most one gradient, in a retro-poster tradition — not a busy scene. Negative space dominates: 70–80% flat color field, with object and title the only marks.

**palette:** One or two flat saturated colors in a hard-edged retro-poster combination (burnt orange + deep navy, or acid green + black), high contrast.

**scrim/legibility habit:** None needed — the flat color field is the background; keep the object simple enough that it never crosses the title's bounding box.

**confidence:** Medium-high — thumbnail-driven minimalism is one of the most consistently cited cross-genre 2025–2026 trends and maps cleanly onto sci-fi's existing retro-poster tradition, though this exact combination is inferred rather than tied to one named bestseller cover.

## 6. `romance_flat_vector_couple`

**genres:** romance

**evidence:** Named foundational/anchor examples cited by The Spokesman-Review (May 2025): *The Hating Game* (Sally Thorne), *The Wedding Date* (Jasmine Guillory), *The Kiss Quotient* (Helen Hoang); Publishers Weekly, "New Trends in Romance Cover Design"; PublishDrive/DAMONZA both describe this as dominant "since roughly 2023" and still dominant through 2025–2026.

**layout:** Title large but not cover-dominating, upper or lower third; author name in a clean band; two fully-clothed figures occupy the middle ground at a "chaste distance," usually full-body, centered or symmetrically flanking a shared prop.

**type:** Quicksand (best fit — rounded, friendly, contemporary geometric sans matching the flat-vector illustration's soft shapes) primary; Playfair Display second choice for a more upmarket romance line. Titles short and punchy, mixed-case or caps, tight-moderate tracking, built for thumbnail readability.

**art:** Two figures in flat vector illustration — simplified faces/bodies, shape-over-texture — on a solid unbroken color background (no gradient scene). Negative space is the flat background itself; keep a clear band above the figures' heads for the title.

**palette:** One or two bright, saturated solid colors as the full background (coral, buttercream yellow, sky blue, lilac) — the cover reads as a single flat color block with figures and type on top.

**scrim/legibility habit:** No scrim — legibility comes from a single flat background hue and an empty color zone reserved directly behind the title.

**confidence:** High — the single most-cited, most stable convention across this entire research pass: multiple independent sources, dated origin (2023), explicit confirmation of continued dominance through 2025–2026, and named bestseller anchors.

## 7. `romcom_maximalist_layered`

**genres:** romance

**evidence:** DAMONZA/Spokesman-sourced statistic: a "60% rise" in "expressive character" illustrated-romance jackets versus 2023; Emily Henry's *Great Big Beautiful Life* (2025, design by Devyn Glista/St. Blanc Studios) as a current bestseller-tier example of the illustrated-romance wave.

**layout:** Title upper or lower third, moderate-to-huge, often integrated with the scene (leaning on a foreground element); one or two characters shown close-up (more detailed than the flat-vector style) placed centrally, with a layered background (foreground florals, mid-ground setting, background sky/skyline).

**type:** Playfair Display primary (its bolder display presence suits busier art); Quicksand second choice to keep it in the same family group as `romance_flat_vector_couple` for a press-wide romance line pairing.

**art:** A close, expressive illustrated character (or couple) with more facial/textural detail than the flat-vector template, set within a full layered scene — foreground floral/object detail, mid-ground character, background setting. Deliberately carve a negative-space gap (patch of sky, blank wall) sized for the title — the busyness is the point, so the text zone must be planned, not assumed.

**palette:** Saturated, warm, multi-color layered palette (sunset corals/pinks/lavenders, or jewel-toned florals) — more color variety than the flat-vector template.

**scrim/legibility habit:** A soft color-graded gap (sky, wall, water) built into the scene behind the title, sometimes reinforced with a subtle drop shadow on the type rather than a hard scrim box.

**confidence:** High — backed by a specific named percentage shift and a current 2025 bestseller example, describing a measured trend rather than a general impression.

## 8. `thriller_bigtype_silhouette`

**genres:** mystery_thriller (thriller)

**evidence:** Multiple corroborating sources (BookCoverZone, "What Makes A Thriller Cover Sell In 2026"; CreativIndieCovers thriller/mystery guide; Rocking Book Covers, "What's Working Now: Thriller/Mystery/Suspense") converge on the same rules, including a cited "silhouettes crush faces 3:1 in conversion rate" claim and "big, bold, capitalized titles dominate the page."

**layout:** Title enormous — up to 70–80% of cover height — centered, upper-two-thirds, tight-tracked caps stacked 1–2 lines; author name (often the real selling point) nearly as large, top or bottom in a contrasting weight; a single silhouette or object placed in a narrow strip below/behind the type, never crossing the letterforms.

**type:** Playfair Display, Black/Bold weight (best available fit — a high-contrast display serif is the closest match in this set to the oversized aggressive title treatment thrillers use) primary; Special Elite second choice for a procedural/investigative, typewritten-evidence register instead of pure boldness.

**art:** One silhouette (a running/standing figure, a profile) or one ominous object (key, door, knife, house) as a hard-edged flat silhouette or single-color graphic — never a detailed scene. Negative space is the majority of the cover, a single flat color field giving the huge type room; the silhouette/object stays confined to its own zone.

**palette:** One dominant dark ground (black, charcoal, navy) plus exactly one "screaming" accent (blood red, acid yellow, electric blue) used only on the title or the object — never both busy at once.

**scrim/legibility habit:** No photographic scrim — legibility is structural: flat dark ground + one light/accent title color + a silhouette confined away from the letterforms.

**confidence:** High — unusually consistent across independent 2025–2026 sources, with specific quantified rules (color counts, conversion stats); arguably the single most locked-in convention in this report.

## 9. `cozy_mystery_graphic_stamp`

**genres:** mystery_thriller (cozy)

**evidence:** Richard Osman's Thursday Murder Club series (latest: *The Impossible Fortune*, 2025) — described in trade coverage as "stark contrast, minimal illustration, slanted letters, and consistent type treatment," explicitly noted as having become influential enough that "many book covers are now being created in Osman style."

**layout:** Title in a bold slanted/italic treatment across the upper third or as a banner through the middle, very large; a small flat graphic icon (teacup, magnifying glass, footprint) in its own clear zone below or beside the title; author name smaller, upright (non-slanted) for contrast.

**type:** Quicksand (best available fit for a rounded, punchy display treatment — set obliqued/italic to approximate the "slanted letters" signature) primary; Special Elite second choice used only as a small stamp-like accent (a "case file" tag or subtitle).

**art:** One small, flat graphic icon or object — never a full scene — in one or two flat colors with a bold outline, sitting in a large empty color field. Negative space dominates (70%+ flat color); icon and title are the only two elements, placed so neither overlaps.

**palette:** Bright and cheerful, explicitly not gritty or moody — warm cream, buttercream yellow, sage green, dusty rose, sky blue as flat blocks.

**scrim/legibility habit:** None needed — the entire cover is a flat color block, so the title is simply set in a contrasting flat ink color.

**confidence:** High — named, current, and explicitly described as having spawned an imitative sub-style across the category, about as strong a dominance signal as this research turns up.

## 10. `cozy_mystery_scenic_illustration`

**genres:** mystery_thriller (cozy)

**evidence:** BookCoversByMelody, "Contemporary Cozy Mystery Cover Trends 2025" and its companion "Paranormal Cozy Mystery Cover Trends 2025" — scenic café/bookshop/village-street illustrations, often with a small cat or dog worked in, bright non-gritty palette explicitly contrasted with adult-crime moodiness.

**layout:** Title in the upper third or as a banner/sign within the illustrated scene (e.g., painted onto a shop awning or floating above rooftops), moderate-large; author name below, smaller. The scene fills the full frame like a storybook illustration.

**type:** Lora (best fit — warm, classically storybook-readable serif) primary; Cormorant Garamond second choice for a more delicate/twee treatment.

**art:** A charming, detailed illustrated scene — village street, bookshop or café interior/exterior, frequently a small cat or dog in the foreground — in a warm, whimsical illustration style (gouache/watercolor-look flats, not photoreal). Plan negative space as sky, a signboard, or a clear wall within the scene, sized to hold the title without fighting the scene's detail.

**palette:** Bright and warm — buttery yellows, warm reds, sage/forest greens, sky blue — deliberately cheerful, avoiding adult crime fiction's grit.

**scrim/legibility habit:** Title sits on a dedicated flat element within the scene (painted sign, awning, open sky) rather than over textured illustration — handled by scene composition, not an added scrim.

**confidence:** High — a dedicated 2025 trend piece documents this with clear, repeatable visual rules distinct from the Osman/graphic-stamp look, confirming these are genuinely two different dominant cozy modes, not duplicates.

## 11. `horror_dark_emblem_ornate`

**genres:** horror, romance (dark-romance crossover)

**evidence:** "Cardinal" (Reedsy, *20 Best Book Covers of 2025*) — a burgundy bird with a blood-red eye surrounded by a sword and spiderwebs, illustrating a "forbidden human-vampire romance," explicitly bridging horror and dark romance; multiple 2025–2026 trend pieces (Troubador, MIBLART, The Rainbow Stories) describe gothic/dark-academia horror covers with ornate borders, dense hidden detail, and jewel-tone-plus-vivid-accent palettes.

**layout:** Title centered, large, sometimes in a slightly distressed/dripping treatment; a single symbolic creature or object (raven, moth, blade, mask) centered above or behind the title, sometimes wrapped in a thin ornate border — sparer and darker than the romantasy emblem.

**type:** Pirata One (best fit — blackletter/gothic display, unmistakably horror-coded) primary; IM FELL English second choice for a more antique "cursed old book" register (period/gothic horror specifically).

**art:** One dark, detailed symbolic emblem (bird, insect, blade, mask, partially obscured figure) with fine linework and hidden micro-detail rewarding close inspection, on an otherwise dark, largely negative-space ground. Keep a clear margin of unbroken dark ground around the emblem so it doesn't collide with border or title.

**palette:** Desaturated dark ground (near-black, deep plum, charcoal) punctuated by one or two vivid jewel-tone or blood-red accents used sparingly (an eye, a drip, a vein of color in the linework).

**scrim/legibility habit:** Title gets its own flat dark band rather than sitting over the emblem's linework; any color accent stays inside the emblem, never behind the letterforms.

**confidence:** High — strongly corroborated across independent trend sources, anchored by a specific, well-described 2025 named cover, with the dark-romance crossover explicitly noted in industry coverage of genre-blending covers.

## 12. `historical_woman_walking_away`

**genres:** historical

**evidence:** Dedicated trend coverage: Book Riot, "Book Cover Trend Watch: WW2-Era Women Looking Away"; BookBrowse, "Faces Lost to Time: The 'Women Looking Away' Book Jacket Phenomenon"; a curated Allegheny County Libraries list built entirely around this convention. Described as "usually set during WWI and WWII," with protagonists "in silhouette, extreme profile, from the back and/or a distance."

**layout:** Title in the upper third (above the figure) or across the bottom margin below her feet, moderate-large, centered or left-aligned; the figure (often with a suitcase, or before a landscape/building) placed off-center per rule-of-thirds, facing away from or across the frame, never toward the viewer.

**type:** EB Garamond (best fit — classic, elegant, unmistakably period) primary; Cormorant Garamond second choice for a softer, more romantic historical register. Title case (not all-caps) is common, signaling literary/upmarket rather than commercial-genre loudness.

**art:** A single figure shown from behind, in silhouette, or at a distance/extreme profile — face hidden or unreadable — against a painterly landscape, period street, or architectural backdrop; illustrated/painterly, visible brushwork, not photographic. When "sense of place" matters more than character, the figure shrinks further and landscape/architecture dominates instead. Negative space is the sky or open landscape around the figure, sized to hold the title without crossing her silhouette.

**palette:** Muted, desaturated period tones — dusty rose, faded teal, sepia, muted gold — evoking old photographs or aged paint rather than bright saturation.

**scrim/legibility habit:** A soft atmospheric haze (mist, dusk light, paint-texture vignette) built into the landscape behind the title zone — an in-world scrim, not a flat overlay box.

**confidence:** High — one of the most explicitly and repeatedly documented single conventions in this entire research pass, with three-plus dedicated trend articles independently naming and describing it identically.

## 13. `literary_minimal_symbolic_object`

**genres:** literary

**evidence:** SPINE Magazine's detailed named 2025 list — *Fools for Love*, *Blob*, *On the Clock*, *Tenterhooks*, *Sea Poison*, *Oddbody*, *Liquid*, *The Wax Child*, *One Day*, *The Lamb*; Sally Rooney's *Intermezzo* (US edition, design by June Park) — a chess-piece/board motif on a yellow-and-gray limited palette with an oversized "old-school Big Book" title font.

**layout:** Title set very large relative to a small central object, positioned top or bottom third (leaving the object floating in the opposite two-thirds); author name small, opposite end from the title. Highly asymmetric, generous flat empty space.

**type:** Spectral (best fit — a contemporary literary serif built for exactly this kind of confident editorial display use) primary; Lora second choice. Title often a single bold weight, mixed-case or caps, sometimes deliberately oversized to near-abstraction.

**art:** One small, often slightly distorted or surreal object (a warped everyday item, a single creature, a fragment) centered on a flat, single-color background — not a scene, just an object and color. Negative space dominates: 80%+ unbroken flat color, with the object and the (large) type the only two marks.

**palette:** Monochrome or near-monochrome — the whole cover in one flat hue (mustard yellow, dusty pink, slate gray, olive), the object rendered in a slightly different tone or outline within that same family.

**scrim/legibility habit:** None needed — flat single-color ground means the title is simply set in a contrasting ink color; the object stays small and off to the side of the title's bounding box rather than behind it.

**confidence:** High — an unusually well-documented trend, with ten-plus named 2025 titles from one detailed source plus independent confirmation via a major, widely covered literary bestseller.

## 14. `literary_painterly_fine_art`

**genres:** literary

**evidence:** "When Things Go Missing" by Deborah J. Brasket (Reedsy, *20 Best Book Covers of 2025*) — built on Paul Klee's public-domain painting "Colorful Architecture," with a whited-out house/silhouette added; the broader "art as cover background" trend independently confirmed by ineedabookcover's "50 Book Covers Featuring Paintings" roundup and multiple 2025–2026 trend pieces describing fine-art-inspired, painterly-textured covers that "lean into emotion, atmosphere, and symbolism rather than realism."

**layout:** Title in the lower third, moderate scale, in a refined serif that "bridges gallery and bookshelf aesthetics"; author name smaller, directly above or below. The painting/artwork fills the full frame edge-to-edge, no border.

**type:** EB Garamond (best fit — timeless, elegant, reads as "art book" rather than commercial fiction) primary; Spectral second choice for a slightly more contemporary edit of the same idea.

**art:** A full-bleed fine-art-style painting (or a manipulated/collaged version of one) — visible brushwork, muted or unusual color choices, some abstraction — as the entire background, sometimes with one added silhouette or graphic shape layered on top. Find negative space within the painting's own composition (a sky, a wall, a plain color passage) rather than adding it after — brief the image generator to "leave the [upper/lower] third relatively flat and uncluttered" as part of the painting itself.

**palette:** Whatever the source painting dictates — often muted, slightly desaturated, art-historical (ochre, sage, dusty blue, terracotta) rather than commercial-saturated color.

**scrim/legibility habit:** A soft "whiting-out" or desaturation of the specific patch of painting behind the title (as in the Klee example) rather than a hard scrim box — leans on the painting having a naturally quiet passage to hold type.

**confidence:** High — a specific, award-recognized 2025 example plus independent corroboration from multiple trend sources describing the same "art as background" pattern as a defining 2025 characteristic.

## 15. `memoir_restrained_object_portrait`

**genres:** memoir_biography

**evidence:** "Childless" by Fabiana Formica (Reedsy, *20 Best Book Covers of 2025*) — the author's face front-and-center with minimal design, negative space beneath forming a "pregnant silhouette" symbolizing creation-and-absence; a general memoir design-philosophy note that "grief and recovery stories carry weight best through restraint — muted palettes, a single charged object, type with room to breathe," versus bolder treatment for survival/comeback stories.

**layout:** Title in a generous, quiet zone — often a full third of the cover left empty around it — centered or left-aligned, moderate scale (not huge); author name (frequently the whole point of a memoir cover) set nearly as prominently as the title, directly below it.

**type:** Spectral (best fit — warm, human, contemporary literary serif) primary; Special Elite as a distinctive second choice for memoirs wanting a "personal testimony/typewritten account" register instead of literary polish (EB Garamond is a solid alternate for a more classic biography).

**art:** Either a simply-cropped portrait treatment (illustrated/graphic, not photographic, per the press's anti-photorealism stance — a bold silhouette or rendered portrait) OR a single charged object standing in for the whole story (a chair, a piece of clothing, a hand). Negative space is the point: at least a third to half the cover should be empty flat color specifically to let the image "breathe" — the opposite of maximalist genres.

**palette:** Muted and restrained — soft neutrals, dusty tones, a single desaturated accent — for grief/reckoning memoirs; brighter/bolder only for survival-and-comeback memoirs, but still limited to one or two colors.

**scrim/legibility habit:** No scrim — restraint itself is the legibility strategy: the image is simple and small enough, and the background flat enough, that text never needs a box or gradient.

**confidence:** High — a specific, award-noted 2025 example plus an explicit, quotable design-philosophy principle (restraint-vs-boldness split by memoir subtype) gives this real texture beyond a generic impression.

## 16. `nonfiction_bold_colorblock_typographic`

**genres:** nonfiction (incl. self-help)

**evidence:** The *Atomic Habits* pattern explicitly cited as the model "popularized by bestsellers" — "massive sans-serif typography paired with a single, high-contrast abstract icon"; "Wall Street's Blind Spots" and "The Happiness Handbook" (both Reedsy, *20 Best Book Covers of 2025*) as concrete named 2025 examples (deep navy/white/gold with bull silhouette; vibrant yellow with white sunburst, respectively); Mel Robbins's *The Let Them Theory* as the #1 bestselling nonfiction/self-help title of 2025, reinforcing the category's current typography-led dominance.

**layout:** Title occupies up to 70–80% of the cover, stacked in heavy weight with tight vertical spacing forming a solid "block" of text, centered or top-third; subtitle small beneath in a lighter weight; author name (often a personal-brand/platform name) at the very bottom or top in its own band.

**type:** Quicksand (best fit among the available families for a friendly, modern, approachable geometric-sans "brand" feel) primary; Playfair Display second choice to signal authority/prestige (business, serious nonfiction) rather than approachability.

**art:** Minimal to none — at most one small, high-contrast abstract icon or silhouette (sunburst, arrow, an object relevant to the thesis) in a single clear zone, never a scene or illustration. Negative space is nearly the whole cover: leave 60–90% as unbroken flat color for the type to own.

**palette:** One saturated, confident flat color as the entire background (electric yellow, cobalt, coral, forest green), title in a single sharply contrasting ink color (white, black, or navy).

**scrim/legibility habit:** None needed — the whole cover is a flat color block by design; legibility is pure color contrast, no layering.

**confidence:** High — the most quantitatively described convention in the whole nonfiction/self-help space (explicit percentage-of-cover figures, a named model book, multiple current named 2025 examples).

## 17. `young_readers_character_illustration`

**genres:** young_readers

**evidence:** The Casual Optimist's extensive "Notable YA Covers of 2025" list (40+ named 2025 titles, almost all illustrated — *After Life* by Gayle Forman, *I Am Not Jessica Chen* by Ann Liang, *Messy* by Tanya Boteju, and others); "Tulip: The Brave Little Beaver" (Reedsy, *20 Best Book Covers of 2025*) for the younger/MG end — a beaver protagonist center-stage with a joyful expression, bright pink/yellow tulips, minimized background.

**layout:** Title in the upper or lower third (whichever the character doesn't occupy), large and bold, often integrated with or slightly overlapping the illustration; author name smaller, opposite end. The character is the clear focal point, centered or slightly off-center — full body and larger for younger readers, closer-cropped for older YA.

**type:** Quicksand (best fit — rounded, warm, all-ages friendly, works from picture-book through younger-YA) primary; Lora second choice for older middle-grade/younger-YA titles wanting more maturity without losing warmth.

**art:** An expressive character (human, animal, or creature) in a dynamic pose hinting at the story's core emotion (joy, curiosity, courage, mischief), illustrated with warmth and a hand-crafted feel (visible brush/pencil texture, soft shading) — never photoreal, never a busy background. Keep the background deliberately simple (one or two flat/soft-gradient colors, minimal setting detail) so the character reads instantly, with a clear zone reserved for the title that doesn't cross the character's face.

**palette:** Bright, primary-leaning, high-energy color (bold pink/yellow/blue combinations) for younger readers; can shift to a duochrome (two dominant colors) approach for older MG/younger-YA crossover titles.

**scrim/legibility habit:** No scrim — the background stays intentionally simple/flat specifically so text never needs one; where the title does cross the illustration, it sits in a part of the background deliberately left empty, never over the character.

**confidence:** High — an unusually large, specific, current named data set (40+ titles from one 2025 roundup) confirms illustration-over-character as overwhelmingly dominant in this category right now.

## 18. `young_readers_bold_graphic_ya`

**genres:** young_readers

**evidence:** AIBookArt, "Young Adult Book Cover Design: The Complete Guide for 2026," and corroborating SPINE/MIBLART coverage describing typography as "a primary design element more than any other genre" in YA, plus "the duochrome trend (two dominant colors)" as having "defined YA covers for years" with "no signs of fading."

**layout:** Title dominates — often the single largest element on the cover, sometimes wrapping around or interacting with a simplified graphic figure — set upper-third or dead-center, huge, bold caps; author name small, bottom edge.

**type:** Playfair Display (best fit for a dramatic, older-skewing YA display treatment) primary; Quicksand second choice for a punchier, more contemporary register.

**art:** Either a simplified/graphic (not detailed-painterly) figure or motif integrated directly with the lettering, or no figure at all — bold shape and color alone doing the work. When art is present it's flat and graphic, closer to a poster than a storybook page. Structure negative space around the two-color duochrome field, with the title claiming the largest single uninterrupted zone on the cover.

**palette:** Exactly two dominant, high-contrast colors (the "duochrome" convention) — e.g., hot pink + black, cobalt + orange — applied as flat fields across art and background alike.

**scrim/legibility habit:** No scrim — the duochrome system is the legibility mechanism itself (title always rendered in whichever of the two colors contrasts most against its local background).

**confidence:** Medium — the underlying observations (typography-forward, duochrome) are well-sourced but general-category statements rather than tied to specific named 2025 bestseller covers, and in practice most real YA covers blend this with `young_readers_character_illustration` rather than using it in pure form. Treat as a secondary/variant option.

## What screams amateur/AI right now

Per the design-press coverage of AI-cover and self-published-cover failure modes (DAMONZA, KDPeasy, BookAutoAI, and general 2025 mistake round-ups), avoid:

- **Off-genre visual/palette signaling** — a cheerful palette on a grim thriller, or vice versa; readers decode genre from color/iconography in under a second, and a mismatch reads as untrained, not "boldly different."
- **AI-anatomy tells** — warped hands, extra/missing fingers, melted faces, nonsensical background text or objects, inconsistent lighting/shadow direction. (This is one reason the press avoids photorealism/photoreal figures entirely — illustrated/flat/graphic styles sidestep the uncanny-valley failure mode.)
- **More than two typefaces on one cover**, or a title that looks "stuck on" rather than integrated with the art's perspective, lighting, and negative space.
- **Weak or default kerning/tracking** — uneven letter-spacing, ignored optical alignment, title that doesn't survive being set at the specified scale.
- **Failing the thumbnail test** — text too small or low-contrast to read at roughly 150×200px (Amazon search-result size); this is cited repeatedly as the single most common indie/AI-cover failure.
- **Cluttered composition with no clear focal point** — too many competing elements, no visual hierarchy, the eye has nowhere to land first.
- **Generic stock-gradient or bokeh backgrounds** with no relationship to the story — a well-known "AI slop" tell.
- **Overly literal, plot-spelling-out imagery** instead of symbol/mood (a generic person standing in for a specific character rather than an object/symbol that captures the book's essence).
- **Low-resolution, stretched, or visibly upscaled source art**; no accounting for bleed, spine text, or back-cover space.
- **Colors that clash or fail to contrast** so the title is genuinely hard to read against its background — the single most avoidable failure and the most common one flagged across sources.
